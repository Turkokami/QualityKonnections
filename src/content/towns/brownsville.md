## What kind of construction market is Brownsville in 2026?

It is a repair-and-replace market on older housing stock sitting next to a genuine industrial build-out.

The Census Bureau put Brownsville at 192,957 people as of 1 July 2025, up about 3.6 percent since 2020, inside a Cameron County of 433,946. That is growth, not a boom, and it does not describe a city full of new subdivisions. The county holds 166,569 housing units at 65.2 percent owner-occupied, median value $136,500; inside the city the median is $139,900 and owner-occupancy runs 61.7 percent. Read that as owners in homes worth well under the national median, deciding whether to fix a roof or replace it.

The industrial side is different. The Brazos Island Harbor Channel Improvement Project at the Port of Brownsville was completed on 25 June 2026, taking the main channel from 42 feet to 52 and the entrance and jetty channels from 44 to 54, at about $295.2 million. The port authority controls more than 40,000 acres and handles 458,800 truck movements a year, and NextDecade took final investment decision on Rio Grande LNG Train 4 in September 2025. Starbase incorporated as a Type C municipality effective 20 May 2025, and House Bill 5246 moved authority over closing Boca Chica Beach and SH 4 to the new city.

So the work splits. Post-war frame and brick ranch homes near the historic core need roofs and drainage fixed. Warehouses, laydown yards and industrial pads need thousands of yards of dirt moved correctly. Our [dirt work and roofing divisions](/services/) exist because most owners here eventually need both.

## Which soil series will your pad actually sit on here?

Mostly vertisols, which is a polite way of saying clay that changes volume with the weather. Four series come up over and over here: Harlingen clay, Benito clay, Chargo silty clay and Laredo silty clay loam.

Harlingen clay is a very-fine smectitic Sodic Haplustert, moderately well drained with very slow permeability, its cracks open between 150 and 210 cumulative days in most years. Lower in the profile, electrical conductivity runs 16 to 20 dS/m. Benito clay is the same taxonomic family but poorly drained, runoff very slow to ponded, slickensides from 8 to 63 inches, cracks 0.4 to 3.0 inches wide reaching 20 inches down, and a seasonal high water table 4 to 6 feet down from September through May. That is why a trench dry in August fills up in January.

Chargo silty clay carries a coefficient of linear extensibility of 0.09 to 0.14, putting it in the very high shrink-swell class. Laredo silty clay loam is the relief: well drained, moderate permeability, non-vertic, low shrink-swell. If your lot maps Laredo the earthwork gets cheaper, and we will say so rather than sell you fill you do not need.

The governing line comes from the Texas Section of ASCE, which defines expansive clay as a weighted plasticity index above 15 with potential volume change over one percent, and computes potential vertical rise per TxDOT Tex-124-E over a 15-foot column. It also carries the rule we quote most: foundations shall not be supported by non-engineered fill.

## How do the resacas change where a pad can go?

They put soft, wet, variable ground exactly where the land looks flattest. Resacas are former channels of the Rio Grande, cut off by Falcon Dam and the flood-control works that came with it, which also stopped new ones forming. The Corps of Engineers evaluated 66 resacas near Brownsville, from under one acre to over 50, averaging about 18. Three cross the urban area: Resaca de la Guerra, Resaca del Rancho Viejo and Town Resaca. Roughly 3,500 acres remain of an estimated 70,000 before agricultural expansion in the 1870s.

They are still working infrastructure: the Brownsville Public Utilities Board manages over 50 miles of the city's historic resaca waterways and roughly 4 million cubic yards of sediment under a 75-year maintenance plan begun in 2013, serving irrigation, raw water supply, stormwater drainage and recreation. Resaca de la Palma lends its name to two places locals mix up: the Battlefield, a National Park Service unit, and the State Park, run by Texas Parks and Wildlife.

The problem is what sits underneath. Geologic mapping here shows Holocene muddy floodplain alluvium and low-permeability Beaumont mud and clay. A lot filled across an old channel margin can have three feet of stiff native clay at one end and eight feet of soft material at the other. Build one pad across that boundary without addressing it and you get differential settlement, which shows up as a diagonal crack from a door corner about two years after closing. The fix is a boring log, a decision about undercutting or bridging, and a plan that treats the lot as two problems.

## How high does a pad have to sit on ground this flat?

Higher than the base flood elevation for your specific lot, plus whatever freeboard your floodplain administrator requires. In a mapped A or AE zone, federal floodplain criteria require the lowest floor at or above the base flood elevation. Freeboard, in FEMA’s words, is a factor of safety expressed in feet above a flood level; most communities choose one to three feet.

Brownsville sits on a low-gradient delta plain, and large parts of it are in mapped flood zones. We deliberately do not publish a percentage for how much of the city lies in the Special Flood Hazard Area, because the sources that would support one are either behind a fetch block or exist only as unsearchable scans.

Harlingen publishes a real local standard in its subdivision ordinance: foundations in Zone X no less than 18 inches above top of curb, a minimum of 24 inches in a Special Flood Hazard Area, and lots within 100 feet of a drainage inlet finished six inches higher than neighbouring lots. Brownsville's numbers come from the plat, the survey and the floodplain administrator, and the [permit and elevation questions](/compliance/) are worth settling before fill trucks are scheduled. Height is not only a flood answer: getting the floor up and the fall away from the slab keeps perimeter clay at a stable moisture content.

## What does 95 percent standard Proctor buy you in this clay?

A fill whose density you can prove, which is different from a fill that looks compacted. Ninety-five percent standard Proctor means 95 percent of the maximum dry density determined by ASTM D698. FEMA's technical bulletin on fill for floodplain construction treats that as roughly equivalent to 90 percent modified under ASTM D1557 for fill supporting buildings, and caps lifts at 12 inches.

In this clay the number that decides the outcome is moisture, not passes. Place vertisol dry of optimum and you can hit density on the gauge and still have a fill that swells when it finally gets wet. Place it wet and it pumps under the roller. Moisture conditioning — discing, watering or drying material until it is in the range the lab specified — is the least glamorous line on the estimate and the one that most often separates a flat slab from a cracked one.

Texas has no statewide codified definition of select fill, and no code-mandated depth. In Gulf Coast geotechnical practice the term usually means a low-plasticity sandy clay or an imported caliche blend, typically in a plasticity-index range of roughly 8 to 18, placed in loose lifts of eight inches or less. Anyone who tells you the code requires 24 inches of select fill under your slab is repeating a rumour. Your engineer specifies the section. We build it and hand you the density tests.

## Why does the base course decide whether a driveway or laydown yard survives?

Because on this ground the base is the structure. Caliche is secondary calcium carbonate formed by leaching and reprecipitation through six recognised morphologic stages; in the Valley it is quarried and crushed as a base material to improve drainage and traction on unpaved roads, and it maps as unit Tc.

The TxDOT flexible base specification is the reference most owners have heard of without knowing what it says. Grade 1 material caps liquid limit at 35 and plasticity index at 10; Grade 2 allows a liquid limit of 40 and a PI of 12. Compaction is specified as not less than 100 percent density by the department's test method, and caliche is normally furnished as a Type B material. We hedge those figures: they came from an adopted municipal copy of the spec, not the department's own document.

Three things go wrong. The subgrade was never proof-rolled, so the base bridges a soft spot that telegraphs through in the first wet season. The section was placed too thin for the axle loads it actually sees. Or the surface has no crown, so water sits, fines migrate and the road washboards. Out toward the port none of that is theoretical, and [our site development work](/services/) starts with the loads rather than with a truck count.

## Who has to sign off on drainage before dirt moves in Cameron County?

More than most owners expect, and finding out late is expensive. Cameron County contains drainage districts numbered 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6, plus irrigation districts including Bayview, Brownsville, Cameron County Irrigation District No. 2 and No. 16, Harlingen and La Feria. Texas Water Code Chapter 58 gives districts rulemaking authority over their easements. If your outfall, crossing or access touches a district facility, the district controls the elevation and the method of that connection.

Cameron County Drainage District No. 1's master drainage plan states plainly that open ditch connections to existing channels are prohibited, and requires utility crossings to cross within 20 degrees of perpendicular and at least five feet below the existing flowline, concrete-protected. An updated plan has since been issued, so confirm current allowance figures and fees with the district before designing to them.

Above that sits the state layer. Construction disturbing one acre or more generally requires authorisation under the TCEQ construction general permit, with a stormwater pollution prevention plan and erosion controls maintained on site. On a residential lot that rarely applies; on a commercial pad off FM 802 or an industrial site near the port it usually does. The [drainage and permitting framework](/compliance/) is worth reading before you set a schedule, because the approval calendar usually determines your start date.

## What does Inland I actually mean for a Brownsville roof?

It means your roof is inside the state windstorm program, at the middle of the three Cameron County design tiers, and a full replacement is certifiable work. The designated catastrophe area covers 14 first-tier coastal counties; Cameron and Willacy are in it. Hidalgo County is not, being second-tier, which is not the same thing. A roof in San Benito is in the program and a roof in Weslaco is not.

Within Cameron County the Texas Department of Insurance splits the coast three ways. Seaward is South Padre Island only. Inland I covers Brownsville, Port Isabel, Los Fresnos, Laguna Vista, Laguna Heights, Rio Hondo, Bayview, Arroyo City, Boca Chica, Indian Lake, Lozano, Southmost and Port Brownsville. Inland II covers Harlingen, San Benito, La Feria, Rancho Viejo, Olmito and the rest of the mid-county communities. The dividing line is U.S. Highway 77, not a distance from water, and TDI assigns the City of Brownsville Inland I status by special rule regardless of which side of that line an address falls on. Drive north past the loop and you cross into Inland II at Olmito and Rancho Viejo.

Numbers are where sites go wrong. The familiar 130, 120 and 110 mph figures for Seaward, Inland I and Inland II are legacy nominal speeds from the 2006 code cycle and ASCE 7-05. Under ASCE 7-10 the ultimate-strength equivalents for the same buildings are 168, 155 and 143 mph. Those are two sets of units describing identical structures, not an increase in the wind. For a project permitted today TDI no longer publishes a per-zone mph figure; design wind speed is determined per structure under ASCE 7, and the engineer of record sets it.

## Why does the WPI-1 have to be filed before the first shingle comes off?

Because once the work is closed in, the cheap path to certification is gone. Texas Insurance Code 2210.2515 requires a WPI-1 application to be filed with the Texas Department of Insurance before work begins. If it is on file and the work is inspected in the open, a TDI inspector or an appointed qualified inspector can certify it, and TDI inspectors charge no fee. Miss that window and the only remaining route is a Texas-licensed professional engineer certifying concealed work through a WPI-2E, leading to a WPI-8-E, at a fee you did not need to spend.

The certificate itself is a WPI-8, a Certificate of Compliance issued by TDI — not by the contractor, the city or TWIA. It comes on the certification of a TDI inspector, an appointed qualified inspector, or a Texas-licensed professional engineer. No roofing company in Texas can self-certify. Once issued, TDI may not rescind it. WPI-3 is dead: TWIA stopped accepting it on 31 May 2020, though plenty of Texas roofing sites still publish it as live advice.

The exemption people misquote most is the small-repair threshold. Roof repairs under 100 square feet that do not involve decking or framing members do not require inspection. That is 100 square feet, not 100 squares, and it is not a dollar figure. A full replacement is never exempt. The code edition also turns on a date: work begun on or after 1 April 2026 is certified to the 2024 IRC and IBC, where work from 1 September 2020 to that date was certified to the 2018 codes.

Coverage is what is at stake. TWIA's language is that without a certificate it lacks evidence the structure conforms to applicable building codes, and the structure may be considered uninsurable and ineligible for coverage with TWIA. We quote that hedge as a hedge. Note also that the city building permit and the windstorm certificate are two separate compliance tracks with different governing codes, so a project can satisfy the city and still fail the certificate. We do not publish Brownsville's currently adopted I-code edition here because we could not confirm it from a primary source. Ask the city's building division, in writing.

## Which shingle and metal systems earn their keep on Brownsville homes?

For the city's brick ranch and masonry-and-stucco stock, a properly fastened architectural shingle and a concealed-fastener standing seam panel are both defensible. The fastening matters more than the brand.

Start with nails. Code requires four fasteners per strip shingle, and six in high-wind zones of 110 mph and above. Because the lowest TDI design tier anywhere in Cameron County is 110, the six-nail pattern applies across the whole county, Brownsville included. The details are not optional: nails must penetrate both the overlay and the underlay portions of the shingle, no nail may land within two inches of a joint or cutout in the shingle beneath, and fasteners should be 11 or 12 gauge with a 3/8-inch head and 3/4 inch of penetration. High nails and shiners are the most common reason a shingle field peels in a downburst.

Bundle ratings are useful but oversold. ASTM D3161 is fan-induced, Class A at 60 mph through Class F at 110. ASTM D7158 compares uplift force to resistance, Class D up to 115 mph through Class H up to 190. Both standards state their results do not directly correlate to wind speeds experienced in service. Impact ratings work the same way: UL 2218 Class 4 is a two-inch steel ball dropped from 20 feet, twice at the same spot, judged on the underside of the mat rather than cosmetic marking, on new material only. GAF disclaims that its impact-resistant shingles are warranted to withstand hail damage. Many insurers do offer a credit — ask yours before paying for the upgrade.

On metal, standing seam floats on slider clips and moves with temperature. Exposed-fastener R-panel is pinned, which limits expansion and contraction and can back the fasteners out over time; those roofs want inspection upwards of twice a year. That is not a reason to reject it. At 3:12 and steeper on agricultural, structural and simpler residential buildings it is often the right call and the better value.

## What goes on the low-slope roofs downtown and out at the port?

Membrane or modified bitumen, chosen by how the building is used rather than by which system has the better brochure. The masonry commercial stock downtown is largely low-slope, and so is the warehouse space serving the port and the bridges.

Slope sets the boundaries. Under IBC Chapter 15, built-up, modified bitumen, thermoset and thermoplastic single-ply all require a minimum quarter-inch-per-foot slope, as does standing seam metal; lapped, non-soldered metal seams without sealant need 3:12. Asphalt shingles need 2:12 minimum, and between 2:12 and 4:12 they require double underlayment.

TPO's limiting factor is heat, not ultraviolet. Research published through NRCA puts utility-grade TPO at roughly 15 years at a sustained 160°F, standard grades at about 25 years at 180°F, and the most heat-stable formulations beyond 35 years at around 200°F, and states that UV ageing does not appear to be an issue for TPO membranes. Heat ageing differs materially between manufacturers, which is why we specify a formulation rather than a category. Modified bitumen we argue on other grounds: redundancy from multiple plies, puncture resistance, tolerance of foot traffic and repairability. We will not quote a service-life range for mod-bit, because no independent industry body publishes one.

Ponding deserves a straight answer. Manufacturer warranty language commonly defines it as water that does not drain or dissipate from the roof surface within 48 hours after precipitation ends. It is not universally a warranty killer: at least one major manufacturer's NDL guarantee covers leaks in ponding areas on TPO and PVC. On Galvalume metal it is an absolute exclusion. For [commercial and industrial buildings](/commercial/) the answer is usually to fix the drainage while the roof is off.

## What does a hot, humid Climate Zone 2A do to the assembly?

It reverses assumptions most crews learned somewhere colder. Cameron County is IECC and ASHRAE Climate Zone 2A, hot-humid, and the prescriptive ceiling value for Zone 2 is R-38. Low-slope roofs under 2:12 over cooled conditioned space are generally required to meet a three-year-aged solar reflectance of at least 0.55 with thermal emittance of at least 0.75, or an aged solar reflectance index of at least 64. Exceptions exist and code editions are paywalled, so confirm what applies to your permit.

Vapour drive matters more. Here it runs inward, from the hot humid outside toward the air-conditioned inside, the opposite of the northern case. That is why Class I vapour retarders rated at 0.1 perm or less are prohibited on the interior side of air-permeable insulation in climate zones 1, 2 and 3A. Staple a polyethylene sheet to the underside of your ceiling joists in Brownsville and you have built a condensing surface inside your own attic.

Unvented attic assemblies in zones 1 through 3 have their own requirement: a vapour diffusion port sized at no less than 1:600 of the ceiling area, located within 12 inches of the ridge, with a vapour permeance of at least 20 perms, on a roof of at least 3:12. Where rigid board insulation is used above the deck, a minimum of R-5 is needed for condensation control. All of it gets skipped on re-roofs where the insulation contractor and the roofer never speak. The attic conversation belongs in the roofing scope, because half those details are inaccessible once the new roof is on.

## How far does salt really reach out SH 4 toward Boca Chica?

The governing number is 1,500 feet, and it comes from warranty documents, not folklore. McElroy Metal's Kynar 500 finish warranty is void for any material installed on property 1,500 feet or fewer from a salt water environment. Galvalume substrate warranties exclude goods installed within 1,500 feet of a salt-water or other marine environment, and MBCI applies the same rule. The Metal Construction Association draws the same line. Every "within five miles of the coast" claim online is drift from that figure.

For most of Brownsville the salt panic is overdone. The bulk of the city sits far beyond 1,500 feet of saltwater, and coated steel is a reasonable specification. Out along the Boca Chica corridor on SH 4, toward the flats and the Gulf, the picture changes and so does the spec.

Two things get corrected constantly. Galvalume is not the coastal metal; that is backwards. It carries no substrate warranty within 1,500 feet of saltwater, and neither it nor galvanised steel will stand up to true coastal conditions. Aluminium is the oceanfront and bayfront substrate, holding 25-year coastal PVDF paint warranties, but it expands roughly twice as much as steel, meaning expansion clips beyond about 15 to 20 feet, it dents more easily, and it costs 15 to 25 percent more. Second, fasteners have to match the substrate. For aluminium panels, 300-series stainless such as 304 is the coastal answer, and Type 410 stainless is explicitly not coastal-rated. Even so, "always stainless" is not universal: DECRA discourages stainless with its own steel systems.

Airborne chlorides are hygroscopic. They hold an electrolyte film long after the rain stops and break down the passive oxide layers on zinc and aluminium, so exposure is about deposition and wash-off, not distance alone. Sheltered undersides corrode faster than rain-washed field panels, and fresh-water rinsing is a real maintenance item.

## What did Beulah, Allen, Dolly and Hanna actually break here?

Roof coverings, on lower-quality construction. That is the documented failure mode across every major storm in this region's record, and the honest argument for spending on the assembly rather than the shingle colour.

Beulah made landfall as a Category 3 between the mouth of the Rio Grande and Brownsville in 1967, measuring 136 mph at the Port of Brownsville. Allen came ashore in 1980 as a Category 3 near Port Mansfield by the Brownsville Weather Service account, with a 12-foot surge and four feet of water standing in Brownsville buildings. The National Hurricane Center's final report on Dolly in 2008 rated it Category 1 at 70 knots, about 81 mph, at mainland landfall, with roughly $1.05 billion in damage, mainly moderate structural damage primarily to roofs on South Padre Island, and 13.20 inches of rain here; the commonly repeated Category 2 at 100 mph figure was the operational estimate, not the final report. Hanna in 2020 came in at Category 1, 80 knots or 92 mph, about $1.2 billion in damage, with a report summary noting hundreds of poorly built structures suffered roof damage.

Day to day the threat is smaller. The Valley averages fewer than 30 thunderstorm days a year and severe storms on under a third of those, with 540 hail and wind events logged between 1996 and 2021, about 20.9 a year, peaking in May. Downbursts are the primary wind threat and occur far more frequently than tornado damage. Hail comes rarely and hits hard: the 2012 mid-Valley storm across Hidalgo County damaged over 1,000 structures for roughly $600 million insured. On the coast, wind is the everyday driver.

## What does each part of Brownsville call us for?

The differences are geographic rather than demographic. Southmost sits low and close to the river with the oldest drainage infrastructure in the city, so the calls there are grading, positive fall away from slabs, and standing water that will not clear after a hard rain. Downtown and the historic district are masonry commercial buildings with low-slope built-up and modified bitumen roofs, where the work is flashing, penetrations and a phased re-roof over an operating business.

West Brownsville, along FM 802 and FM 1732, is newer tract housing on former farmland: engineered pads on agricultural clay, subdivision drainage that has to meet a plat, and roofing on a builder's schedule. The Rancho Viejo corridor north of the loop has golf-course subdivisions built partly on filled resaca ground, exactly the differential-settlement case described earlier, and it is where the design tier changes at the municipal boundary. The Boca Chica corridor east toward the Gulf gives the most aggressive exposure in the county.

Reference points, not a radius map: Palo Alto Battlefield National Historical Park to the north, Sabal Palm Sanctuary on the river bend, the Gateway International Bridge and the Veterans International Bridge at Los Tomates funnelling cross-border freight. Work in [the rest of Cameron County](/locations/) follows different rules, sometimes a few hundred yards away.

Two boundaries close this out. Texas does not license roofing contractors at all — TDLR's regulated programs do not include roofing — so the word "licensed" on a Texas roofing website means little on its own, and RCAT's program is a voluntary industry credential rather than a state licence. And while we build to the certifiable specification and coordinate the inspection, we do not issue certificates, set base flood elevations, sign elevation certificates or perform engineering.
